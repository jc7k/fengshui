# Glossary

Plain-language definitions, cross-linked to the primers where each idea is taught.

- **Bagua (八卦)** — the "eight symbols" map that divides a space into nine life areas. See
  [[../01-Feng-Shui-Fundamentals/The-Eight-Trigrams-and-Bagua|the primer]].
- **Ba Zhai (八宅)** — "Eight Mansions," the compass method that gives each *person* four good and
  four bad directions from their Kua. See [[../04-Week-3-Eight-Mansions/Eight-Mansions-Ba-Zhai|Week 3]].
- **Ba Zhai Ming Jing (八宅明鏡)** — "Bright Mirror of the Eight Mansions," the school's canonical
  text: traditionally attributed to the Tang master Yang Yunsong, but compiled in the Qing dynasty
  (best-known edition 1790) — sources disagree on how much is his. See
  [[../04-Week-3-Eight-Mansions/Eight-Mansions-Ba-Zhai|Week 3]].
- **Ba Zi (八字)** — "Four Pillars," a Chinese-astrology reading of a birth moment. We build only a
  taster (the year pillar). See [[../09-Bonus-BaZi/Four-Pillars-Ba-Zi|the Ba Zi primer]].
- **BTB** — Black (Hat) Sect Tantric Buddhism, Professor Lin Yun's 1970s Western adaptation that
  aligns the bagua to the front door instead of the compass. See [[../07-Week-6-BTB-Contrast/BTB-and-Compass-Bagua|Week 6]].
- **Chi / Qi (氣)** — "life energy" or "vital breath"; the thing feng shui tries to keep flowing
  well. See [[../01-Feng-Shui-Fundamentals/Chi-Yin-Yang|the primer]].
- **Dragon veins (龍脈, lóng mài)** — mountain ranges read as living conduits of chi; the
  landscape-scale object of classical Form School analysis. See
  [[../06-Week-5-Form-School/Form-School-and-the-Four-Animals|Week 5]].
- **Early Heaven bagua (先天八卦)** — Fu Xi's symmetric "ideal cosmos" trigram arrangement (a
  traditional attribution); today seen mostly on protective bagua mirrors over doorways. See
  [[../07-Week-6-BTB-Contrast/BTB-and-Compass-Bagua|Week 6]].
- **Earthly Branches (地支)** — the sexagenary cycle's 12-wheel (子 … 亥); each branch carries a 生肖
  zodiac animal — the animals are the branch system's pop-culture surface, not the system itself.
  See [[../09-Bonus-BaZi/Four-Pillars-Ba-Zi|the Ba Zi primer]].
- **Eight qualities (Ba Zhai)** — the labels each Kua's eight directions receive: auspicious Sheng Qi
  (生氣), Tian Yi (天醫), Yan Nian (延年), Fu Wei (伏位); inauspicious Huo Hai (禍害), Wu Gui (五鬼),
  Liu Sha (六煞), Jue Ming (絕命). See [[../04-Week-3-Eight-Mansions/Eight-Mansions-Ba-Zhai|Week 3]].
- **Facing direction** — the compass bearing the front of a building "addresses." Genuinely hard to
  measure; competing definitions. Stored as `facing_degrees` in each home.
- **Feng Shui (風水)** — "wind–water," named from the *Zangshu*'s teaching that qi rides the wind and
  scatters, and halts when it meets water. See [[../02-Week-1-Whole-Game/Feng-Shui-Origins-Wind-and-Water|the origins note]].
- **Flying Stars (Xuan Kong Fei Xing, 玄空飛星)** — the time-based compass school that gives a
  *building* a 9-palace natal chart. See [[../05-Week-4-Flying-Stars/Flying-Stars-Xuan-Kong|Week 4]].
- **Form School (Luan Tou, 巒頭)** — the oldest school; reads landscape and the shape of space.
  See [[../06-Week-5-Form-School/Form-School-and-the-Four-Animals|Week 5]].
- **Four Symbols (四象)** — the four sky-quadrant guardians of Chinese astronomy behind the Four
  Celestial Animals: 玄武 Xuán Wǔ (Black Tortoise, N), 朱雀 Zhū Què (Red Phoenix, S), 青龍 Qīng Lóng
  (Green Dragon, E), 白虎 Bái Hǔ (White Tiger, W). See [[../06-Week-5-Form-School/Form-School-and-the-Four-Animals|Week 5]].
- **Ganzhi / sexagenary cycle (干支)** — the 60-term cycle made by pairing the 10 Heavenly Stems with
  the 12 Earthly Branches (60 = lcm(10, 12); only same-polarity pairs occur). Used to count *days* on
  Shang oracle bones long before years. See [[../09-Bonus-BaZi/Four-Pillars-Ba-Zi|the Ba Zi primer]].
- **Heavenly Stems (天干)** — the sexagenary cycle's 10-wheel; each stem carries a Five-Elements
  element and a yin/yang polarity (甲 yang Wood … 癸 yin Water). See
  [[../09-Bonus-BaZi/Four-Pillars-Ba-Zi|the Ba Zi primer]].
- **Hong Fan (洪範)** — the "Great Plan" chapter of the *Book of Documents*, the earliest canonical
  listing of the five phases (exact date disputed). See [[../03-Week-2-Elements-and-Bagua/Five-Elements-in-Depth-Wu-Xing|the deep-dive]].
- **Kanyu (堪輿)** — the older, Han-era name for the siting art (roughly "heaven and earth"), used for
  graves and settlements long before floor plans. See [[../02-Week-1-Whole-Game/Feng-Shui-Origins-Wind-and-Water|the origins note]].
- **Kua / Ming Gua number** — a number 1–9 from your birth year and gender that sorts you into the
  East or West group. See [[../04-Week-3-Eight-Mansions/Eight-Mansions-Ba-Zhai|Week 3]].
- **Later Heaven bagua (後天八卦)** — King Wen's "world in motion" arrangement (traditional
  attribution); the trigram layout every compass method uses — why Fame/Li sits South. See
  [[../07-Week-6-BTB-Contrast/BTB-and-Compass-Bagua|Week 6]].
- **Li Chun (立春)** — "start of spring," ~Feb 4; the real start of the feng shui year. Births before
  it belong to the previous year.
- **Li Qi (理氣)** — "principle/regulating qi," the umbrella term for all **compass-method** feng shui
  (Ba Zhai, Xuan Kong, San He…), as opposed to form work. See
  [[../08-Week-7-8-Capstone/How-Practitioners-Reconcile-the-Schools|the Capstone primer]].
- **Lo Shu (洛書)** — the 3×3 magic square whose "flight path" the Flying Stars follow.
- **Luopan (羅盤)** — the feng shui compass. We use two of its rings: the 8 directions and the 24
  mountains. See [[../01-Feng-Shui-Fundamentals/The-Luopan-and-24-Mountains|the primer]].
- **Ming Tang (明堂)** — the "bright hall," the open space in front of (or just inside) the main door
  where chi gathers and settles before entering. See [[../06-Week-5-Form-School/Form-School-and-the-Four-Animals|Week 5]].
- **Mountain (24 Mountains)** — one of 24 named 15° compass slices, each tagged yin or yang.
- **Missing corner** — a bagua sector with no physical space in the home (an L-shaped plan).
- **Command position** — placing a bed/desk to see the door without sitting in its direct line.
- **Poison arrow (sha chi)** — chi rushing in a straight line at a spot (a corridor, a door aligned
  with a bed, an outside road or roof edge).
- **Qi kou (氣口)** — the "mouth of chi": the front door, where chi (and everyone) enters; BTB's
  anchor point. See [[../07-Week-6-BTB-Contrast/BTB-and-Compass-Bagua|Week 6]].
- **San He (三合)** — "Three Harmonies," the compass-school camp whose formulas key off fixed
  directions and surrounding landforms, largely ignoring time. Rival camp to San Yuan. See
  [[../08-Week-7-8-Capstone/How-Practitioners-Reconcile-the-Schools|the Capstone primer]].
- **San Yuan (三元)** — "Three Cycles," the compass-school camp that puts *time* at the center, and
  its 180-year clock behind Flying Stars: three 60-year cycles, each of three 20-year periods (the
  current one runs 1864–2043). See [[../05-Week-4-Flying-Stars/Flying-Stars-Xuan-Kong|Week 4]] and
  [[../08-Week-7-8-Capstone/How-Practitioners-Reconcile-the-Schools|the Capstone primer]].
- **Tai Sui (太歲) & annual afflictions** — yearly compass zones (Grand Duke, Year Breaker 歲破,
  Three Killings 三煞, annual 5 Yellow) that practitioners avoid disturbing. Covered in
  [[Deep-Research-Study-Guide|the deep-research study guide]]; our code computes natal charts only.
- **Timely star (旺 wàng)** — the star matching the current period (9 in 2024–2043), read as
  prosperous; 生 *shēng* (1) is growing, 退 *tuì* (8) retreating. See
  [[../05-Week-4-Flying-Stars/Flying-Stars-Xuan-Kong|Week 4]].
- **Trigram** — a three-line I Ching symbol (yang = solid, yin = broken); the eight trigrams sit at
  the eight bagua directions.
- **Wu Xing (五行)** — the Five Elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) and their generating and
  controlling cycles. See [[../01-Feng-Shui-Fundamentals/The-Five-Elements-and-Cycles|the primer]].
- **Xie / weakening cycle (洩)** — the generating cycle read backwards: producing a child drains the
  mother (Fire drains Wood). Not implemented in our code — a deliberate scope line. See
  [[../03-Week-2-Elements-and-Bagua/Five-Elements-in-Depth-Wu-Xing|the deep-dive]].
- **Yin / Yang (陰陽)** — the two complementary qualities of chi (still/receptive vs. active/bright).
- **Zangshu (葬書)** — the "Book of Burial," traditionally attributed to Guo Pu (4th c. CE; attribution
  contested), the text that gave feng shui its name. See [[../02-Week-1-Whole-Game/Feng-Shui-Origins-Wind-and-Water|the origins note]].
- **Zou Yan (鄒衍)** — Warring States scholar (c. 305–240 BCE) who systematized the five phases and
  yoked them to yin-yang and dynastic politics; his writings survive only in quotation. See
  [[../03-Week-2-Elements-and-Bagua/Five-Elements-in-Depth-Wu-Xing|the deep-dive]].
